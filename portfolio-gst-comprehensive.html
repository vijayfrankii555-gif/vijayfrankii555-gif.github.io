GSTR-1 Filing Timing

The GST Portal's GSTR-1 guidance states that the normal due date is the 11th of the succeeding month for monthly filers and the 13th of the month following the quarter for quarterly filers, subject to extensions by notification.

3. GSTR-1 — Outward Supply Reporting

GSTR-1 is the statement used to report outward supplies. The GST Portal describes B2B supplies, B2C supplies, exports, credit/debit notes, advances, amendments, nil/exempt/non-GST supplies and HSN/SAC summaries among the reported information.

Example Transaction Taxable Value GST GSTR-1 Treatment
B2B Inter-State Sale ₹1,00,000 IGST ₹18,000 B2B invoice reporting
B2C Intra-State Sale ₹50,000 CGST ₹4,500 + SGST ₹4,500 B2C summary reporting
Export on Payment of Tax ₹2,00,000 Illustrative IGST ₹36,000 Export invoice reporting
Credit Note -₹10,000 Illustrative tax adjustment Credit note reporting

From May 2025 onwards, the GST Portal says the HSN-wise summary in GSTR-1 is presented separately for B2B and B2C supplies.

5. GSTR-1A — Example Correction Workflow

GSTR-1A is an optional facility for correcting or adding records for the same tax period. The GST Portal states that it can be used after GSTR-1 or its due date, whichever is later, and before filing GSTR-3B for that period.

Original GSTR-1: Invoice INV-1005 = ₹60,000 taxable value Error identified: Correct taxable value = ₹65,000 GSTR-1A: Add / amend the same-period record GSTR-3B: Corrected liability flows into the reporting process

6. GSTR-2B — ITC Reconciliation

GSTR-2B is a static ITC statement. The GST Portal advises taxpayers to reconcile GSTR-2B with their books and ensure that ITC is not claimed twice, required reversals are made, and reverse-charge tax is paid where applicable.

Books ITC ₹8,000
GSTR-2B ITC ₹7,500
Difference ₹500
Action REVIEW

Example Reconciliation

Books eligible ITC = ₹8,000 GSTR-2B reflected ITC = ₹7,500 Difference = ₹500 Action: Identify missing / timing / eligibility issue before final ITC claim.

10. TDS — Current 2026 Framework

For transactions on or after 1 April 2026, TDS obligations are governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025. The Income Tax Department states that the earlier TDS provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961 were consolidated mainly under sections 392 (salary) and 393 (other specified payments).

For the transition around 31 March / 1 April 2026, the applicable Act depends on the earlier of the credit or payment event.

Important 2026 change: Do not automatically use old section numbers such as 194C or 194J for transactions on or after 1 April 2026. The Income Tax Department states that the relevant table item under section 393 should be used for applicable post-1-April-2026 payments.

11. TDS — Contractor / Professional Example

Use the applicable statutory rate and threshold for the exact transaction. The calculation below uses a clearly labelled illustrative rate so that the arithmetic can be understood without treating the example rate as a legal rate.

TDS = Amount liable to TDS × Applicable TDS Rate Example: ₹50,000 × 10% = ₹5,000
